# Transformer Inverse Design at 15 GHz with Discrete Quality Factor Selection

## Abstract

We study single frequency transformer inverse design with discrete quality-factor selection. A tandem multilayer perceptron generates eleven candidates and a frozen forward model selects one before electromagnetic simulation. A 6329-geometry development view contains 3801 training, 1269 validation and 1259 held-out test examples. Five network configurations were trained with three seeds. The frozen three-layer 256-unit seed-17 pair was subsequently evaluated on the held-out set without model reselection; 1253/1259 inverse outputs meet the joint tolerance under SELF_PROXY evaluation, not physical validation. Its separate 128-request physical pilot is now terminal: 40/128 requests attain all four targets (31.25%), or 40/48 (83.33%) conditional on strict validity. These are different denominators, not interchangeable model-accuracy scores. The 23-case external forward diagnostic remains conditional on excluded historical-pilot geometries. A separate historical-reference pilot completed 64 requests: 23 are strictly valid and 17 meet the joint tolerance, giving 26.56% over the original denominator; 35 extracted responses fail the half-self-resonance condition. A matched sixteen-attempt-per-arm acquisition prefix adds one previously empty training-response cell for directed proposals and none for geometry controls. These development observations do not establish superiority or final-model physical accuracy.

## Index Terms

Integrated transformers, inverse design, neural networks, electromagnetic simulation, quality factor.

## Introduction

Transformer inverse design must connect a requested electrical response to a geometry that can be manufactured and evaluated consistently. A forward predictor can approximate the response of a known geometry, whereas an inverse predictor must select a geometry from a potentially nonunique solution set. Agreement between the two networks is useful for screening, but it does not establish that the proposed structure realizes the desired response. The distinction becomes important when a request lies in a sparsely sampled region or when a continuous network output changes after manufacturing-grid conversion.

Learning-assisted synthesis of transformer-based matching networks has already been reported. Er et al. studied end-to-end synthesis of millimeter-wave passive matching networks with three-dimensional electromagnetic structures [1]. Chu, Mao and Wang studied transfer-learning-assisted migration of transformer matching networks across technology nodes [2]. These precedents motivate the use of learned mappings but do not establish accuracy for the component-response task considered here. We do not claim the first neural transformer inverse model, a new network architecture, or a demonstrated improvement over either study. Their circuit objectives and reported metrics are not interchangeable with our response errors.

Our task is deliberately single frequency. At 15 GHz, a user fixes the primary inductance, secondary inductance and magnitude of coupling. The quality factor target is chosen from the integers ten through twenty by comparing all four response errors symmetrically. Selecting the largest quality factor would solve a different problem. Likewise, accepting every design above a quality-factor threshold would discard the prescribed target-matching interpretation. We retain the same scoring convention throughout candidate generation, selection and physical evaluation.

The intended contribution is an experimentally supported account of response-space coverage, simple tandem modeling and preselected-design electromagnetic verification. Three questions guide the work. First, how much compatible historical evidence actually lies inside the joint target domain? Second, does supplementing sparse response regions improve coverage and prediction under controlled budgets? Third, how reliably does the proxy-selected candidate meet its own four targets after geometry export and electromagnetic analysis? Historical coverage remains partially audited. The frozen-reference pilot provides initial electromagnetic-simulation evidence addressing the third question; an initial matched acquisition prefix now supplies limited coverage evidence, while full controlled supplementation and final-model validation remain incomplete.

## Design Domain and Electromagnetic Evidence

### Electrical specification and validity

Let the electrical response be y = (Lp, Ls, Qmin, |k|), with Qmin = min(Qp, Qs). The original primary and secondary quality factors and signed coupling are retained in the data record. The requested inductance interval is 0.5 to 2.0 nH for each winding, and the coupling-magnitude interval is 0.2 to 0.85; all boundaries are inclusive. A design request fixes the three inductance-coupling coordinates. For each integer q from 10 through 20, its associated four-target vector is y(q) = (Lp, Ls, q, |k|).

These bounds define a sampling window, not a proven rectangular feasible region. Joint combinations can be poorly supported even when every individual coordinate falls inside a previously observed marginal range. A missing training example is not evidence that the corresponding device is physically impossible. Conversely, proximity to an observed point in a two-dimensional projection does not prove that all four requested quantities can be achieved together. Response-space occupancy and per-request attainment are therefore evaluated separately.

The retained native workflow exports actual Cadence geometry, checks the foundry geometry and port contract, applies the existing Calibre rule scope, and simulates the same audited GDS with EMX. The preserved contract uses four single-ended signal ports with grounded auxiliary connections, a 50-ohm reference and the original extraction port ordering. A zero-blocking Calibre result is required under the existing macro/IP back-end rule scope; it is not a claim of full-chip signoff. Geometry identity, GDS identity and electromagnetic output identity are recorded separately rather than treated as synonyms.

Although the learned response is evaluated at 15 GHz, the simulation evidence retains the existing 5 to 60 GHz sweep with a 1 GHz increment, giving 56 frequency points. This distinction preserves the spectral evidence used by the label extractor, including its below-half-self-resonance criterion. A calculation at 15 GHz alone cannot establish a self-resonance above 30 GHz. We do not alter the production extraction rule, expand its validity claims or relabel a single-frequency calculation as a complete accepted sweep.

Strict eligibility is evaluated on the actual 15 GHz row. It requires finite physical labels and the executed strict-valid flag before the joint inductance-coupling bounds are applied. In the audited snapshot, the strict flag agrees with the conjunction of descriptor validity and the below-half-self-resonance flag. The intersection with 10 <= Qmin <= 20 is counted separately. Keeping these filters separate avoids silently removing valid evidence outside the quality-factor scan interval that may be useful for forward-model diagnostics or an explicitly defined training guard band.

### Historical reuse and development partitions

The current quantitative audit covers one frozen source snapshot with 10000 unique geometries. Its source geometry partitions contain 6015 training, 2002 validation and 1983 test members. After the original 15 GHz strict filter, the corresponding eligible counts are 3175, 1006 and 1046, totaling 5227. The term source snapshot is important: ten thousand source geometries do not mean ten thousand gradient-training examples. The reference model uses the original strict training population, not a retrospective restriction to the new domain.

Applying the new joint domain to that snapshot yields 1804 training, 595 validation and 619 test examples, totaling 3018. Its quality-factor intersection totals 3016. The later development view uses 6329 formal-production geometries: 6281 previously audited members plus 48 accepted additions. It retains the original 3018 memberships and assigns 3801 training, 1269 validation and 1259 test examples. The 348 historical-research members and 23 strictly valid original-pilot outputs are excluded. Normalization uses training rows only. This is a frozen development dataset, not all historical evidence, the entire 6700-member owner ledger, or a FINAL dataset.

The current split preserves canonical geometry identity, but parent-family independence is not certified because the consumed records lack a parent mapping. A final dataset must also group related local perturbations. Normalizers, coverage proposals and any sampling weights are learned from training data only. Historical test sets already consulted during development remain labeled historical; they cannot become an untouched final benchmark by renaming their files. New-domain pilot feedback may guide development, but the final physical requests and their outcomes will not be used to update the same final model being evaluated.

### Response coverage and supplementation

For the original 1804-training-example acquisition baseline, the inductance-coupling domain is partitioned into a fixed 8 by 8 by 8 grid. The 1804 strict in-domain training examples occupy 147 of the 512 cells, leaving 365 empty. A coarser 4 by 4 by 4 grid is retained as a sensitivity view. These occupancy counts describe the empirical training distribution at a stated resolution. They are not estimates of the fraction of all physically achievable devices, and an occupied cell does not imply that every target within it is reachable.

Quality-factor coverage is also conditioned on the joint domain. In the in-domain training population, the neighborhood from 18.5 inclusive to 19.5 exclusive contains two examples; the neighborhood from 19.5 inclusive to 20.5 exclusive contains none. These are neighborhood counts, not counts of labels exactly equal to the integers nineteen or twenty. Consequently, candidate generation near the upper end of the scan must expose limited support instead of clipping the target and claiming the original specification was met.

The first supplementation comparison freezes 128 directed proposals and 128 geometry Latin-hypercube controls. Cells with fewer than five original training observations receive weight max(5 - count, 0); 102 distinct cells are sampled without replacement, with uniform targets inside each. Twenty-six geometry proposals provide exploration. Seeds 2026090901 through 2026090904 fix selection, exploration, controls and order. Of 256 proposals, 203 are eligible and 53 remain unreplaced holds. The closed prefix contains 16 solver-started attempts per arm, retaining invalid outcomes. Directed proposals yield seven strictly valid responses, four in-domain, two train-split additions and one newly occupied cell; controls yield ten, eight, four and zero, respectively. Occupancy changes from 147 to 148 versus 147 to 147. Recorded native elapsed totals are 1046.78 versus 1077.95 s. Coverage uses actual labels and the original 1804 training baseline. This admission-conditional prefix is not equal proposal efficiency, global wall time, completed 128-per-arm performance or evidence of statistical superiority. A separate fixed thirteen-outcome acquisition increment contains eleven extracted responses and two GDS failures: three responses are strict-valid and eight fail the half-self-resonance condition. This unbalanced increment is not a matched-budget comparison, establishes no coverage gain or acquisition winner, and admits no rows into training.

## Tandem Reference and Discrete Selection

### Implemented model and optimization

The current reference consists of a forward network with dimensions 10 to 256 to 256 to 256 to 4 and an inverse network with dimensions 4 to 256 to 256 to 256 to 10. Each hidden layer uses the existing tanh-approximation Gaussian error linear unit. The two networks have 135428 and 135434 trainable parameters, respectively. Frequency selects the package and is not an extra neural input. In contract order, the geometry coordinates are primary outer width and height, secondary outer width and height, line width, primary and secondary terminal-y spans, offset, and primary and secondary feed extensions, all in micrometers.

The inverse decoder applies an independent sigmoid to each bounded logit and maps it into the observed standardized training-geometry envelope before conversion back to physical units. This coordinatewise envelope is not a guarantee of coupled geometric feasibility. The exported geometry is rounded to the original 0.005 micrometer grid. Rounding occurs for export and scoring; it is not a differentiable straight-through operation in the tandem training graph.

Geometry and response normalizers are fitted on the eligible training subset. The forward objective uses standardized response residuals with weights derived from the ratio of training-label scales to the declared response spans. The weights are normalized to unit mean. It is therefore proportional to a fixed-span response mean-squared error, rather than an unqualified unweighted error in physical units. Normalized training loss and reported physical errors are kept distinct so that a small optimization loss is not presented as an electrical accuracy percentage.

We adopt the tandem principle demonstrated for nanophotonic inverse design by Liu et al. [3]. The forward network is trained first and its validation-selected checkpoint is frozen for inverse training. Freezing parameters does not freeze the response as a function of generated geometry. The inverse loss must retain the derivative through the forward network to the decoder output. The existing implementation combines a response-consistency term with a geometry-normalized auxiliary reconstruction term. The geometry term regularizes the inverse mapping; recovering the original training geometry is not itself the definition of inverse-design success because multiple geometries may realize similar responses.

The audited reference recipe uses a scheduled response weight and a geometry-term coefficient of 0.01. The response weight has a warm-up and ramp before the existing moving-average adaptation; the adaptation is associated with validation events, not every optimizer step. Validation checkpoint selection uses its documented response and geometry criterion rather than the instantaneous weighted training loss. These details are retained for reference replay. The current development study retains this recipe. Its inverse checkpoint criterion is the square root of response loss plus 0.01 times the square root of standardized geometry-anchor loss, without the scheduled response multiplier.

The recorded reference recipe uses AdamW with learning rate 0.0003, weight decay 0.0001, constant learning rate and gradient-norm clipping at one. An effective batch of 32 is accumulated from microbatches of eight, sampling eligible training geometries uniformly with replacement. The reference seed is 17; execution used a CPU with two threads. The nominal budget is 12000 updates per network, with validation every 100 updates and at the first update, subject to the saved time, exposure and early-stopping limits. Optimizer, random-generator and scheduling states are retained for continuation.

For the formal reference, the selected forward and inverse checkpoints occur at updates 11800 and 11900; the retained last checkpoints occur at update 12000 for both. The saved status is partial rather than an assertion of convergence. Separate development-5000 and formal-10000 source references have been loaded and identified without merging their physical scores. The reference audit also confirmed a nonzero inverse-training response gradient through the frozen forward. That check verifies implementation behavior, not the physical correctness of generated structures.

### Selection before electromagnetic evaluation

For each requested triple, the inverse network generates eleven continuous geometries. The original export mapping produces the manufacturing-grid candidates, and the frozen forward predicts the response of those exported candidates. The main score is defined by E(q) = sqrt(mean over j of ((Fj(ggrid(q)) - yj(q))/sj) squared), where s = (2.5 nH, 2.5 nH, 20, 0.8). The declared spans are retained historical scales; they are not the widths of the new target box and are not fitted to the evaluation requests.

The chosen qproxy is the integer with the smallest finite score; exact ties use the smaller integer. The finite-score contract is checked before selection. All eleven original predictions, continuous and grid geometries, scores and ordering remain available for audit. Selection does not maximize Qmin and does not treat excess quality factor as cost-free. Because the target quality factor changes with q, the result means the best four-target match among the prescribed alternatives, not the best response to one unchanged four-dimensional target.

The preselected identity is fixed before native evaluation. An analytically invalid selected geometry is retained as a failed request and is not replaced by another Q that passes. Subsequent export, DRC or solver failure also cannot trigger an unrecorded reranking. The ten unselected candidates remain proxy evidence and are marked as not requested in the main physical trial. This makes the all-request result interpretable as the performance of the stated selection procedure, including its failures.

The score used for selection and the criterion for simultaneous target attainment are different quantities. For compatibility, the legacy absolute tolerance vector is (0.125 nH, 0.125 nH, 1, 0.04). This is not a requirement that each response be within five percent of its own target. The implementation records the exact floating-point thresholds used at boundary comparisons. Physical-unit errors and target-relative errors will be reported alongside tolerance-dependent hit rates; no accuracy score is formed by subtracting a normalized error from one.

## Evaluation Design and Controlled Comparisons

### Forward and inverse evidence

Forward prediction is compared with electromagnetic labels from geometries excluded from fitting and checkpoint selection. The completed 1259-case development holdout uses previously saved EM labels; it is separate from the external diagnostic on 23 strictly valid historical-pilot geometries excluded from the 6329-view dataset. Historical Q selection and strict-survivor selection preclude a representative random-test interpretation for the latter. Feeding an inverse-generated geometry back into its scoring forward instead gives a SELF_PROXY residual. In the separate historical64 and development128 physical pilots, EM-minus-target measures request realization, whereas EM-minus-frozen-forward measures surrogate discrepancy. The holdout, both pilots and their model identities are not pooled; final independent model validation remains future work.

For each feature, the planned summary includes signed bias, mean absolute error, root-mean-square error, median absolute error, upper absolute-error percentiles and the maximum observed absolute error. Inverse target-relative errors use the nonzero requested value; external-forward percentage errors use the absolute prior EM label. Conditional numerical summaries use only requests with the required defined labels, with that sample count displayed. Every summary is accompanied by the original-request denominator, valid-result fraction and stage outcomes. Missing or invalid labels are not imputed as zero error or assigned an arbitrary hundred-percent error.

Analytical or physical rejection means the procedure has not demonstrated satisfaction of the request. Resource waiting and unfinished work are instead reported as pending; they are not evidence of device infeasibility. In a partially completed campaign, the observed all-request attainment count is distinguishable from a completed-campaign success probability. An absolute-error ninety-fifth percentile is a property of the observed errors, not a ninety-five-percent confidence bound or a maximum-error guarantee. Multiple Q candidates within one request are not independent test requests.

### Final random requests and the full scan subset

After final data and model selection are frozen, the planned main evaluation draws 10000 independent uniform inductance-coupling triples using a recorded seed. All requests are created before the incremental campaign advances through the same list. Eleven proxy evaluations per request give 110000 candidate evaluations, but only one preselected candidate per request is admitted to the main native workflow. The main budget is therefore at most 10000 new solves before failures and exact reuse, not 110000 physical solves.

A subset of 100 requests will be randomly chosen before physical results are observed for a complete eleven-Q audit. Since their selected candidates also belong to the main trial, this adds at most 1000 candidate solves. The physical best-in-library Q is defined only for a request whose eleven original candidates all satisfy the required strict-valid conditions. Otherwise the missing or failed candidates remain visible and no full-library optimum is declared. This audit result does not replace the original qproxy in the main statistics.

Exact reuse requires identical geometry and physical configuration with validated evidence. Requests, distinct geometries, native solves, cache hits and fresh-result fractions will be counted separately. If two requests produce the same geometry, they remain distinct request outcomes but do not become two independent physical realizations. A retained known-reachable panel from independent electromagnetic geometries will be reported separately: changing its original quality-factor target into a scan does not automatically preserve the reachability guarantee of the original four-tuple.

### Network depth and width

The completed development study uses two layers of 256 units; three layers of 128, 256 or 512 units; and five layers of 256 units, each with seeds 17, 29 and 43. All models start from scratch on the same 3801 training geometries, with common labels, train-only normalization, activation and evaluation. The per-network cap is 23757 updates, with validation every 100 and patience of 20 validation events. With replacement sampling, this is a 200 draw-equivalent epoch budget, not 200 shuffled passes. Actual stopping steps differ. The retained PARTIAL receipts do not establish sufficient convergence, and the earlier 3018 experiment is not a same-update-budget data-size control.

Each configuration trains its own forward regressor. All inverse networks train through the same frozen validation-best forward from the new 6329-view three-layer 256-unit seed-17 baseline, while preserving gradients with respect to generated geometry. Thus inverse seed dispersion is conditional on one shared surrogate, not complete-system forward-and-inverse uncertainty. The same 1269 validation geometries select checkpoints and provide the architecture diagnostics. The 1259 test geometries were subsequently evaluated once for the pre-frozen three-layer 256-unit seed-17 pair, not to rank all configurations. That evaluation made no new model selection or training update. Neither the holdout results nor the development128 physical outcomes are used to reselect the reported pair; the separate historical-reference weights remain unchanged.

The Q-selection comparison will distinguish fixed Q15, the preselected qproxy procedure and the complete-EM subset. Comparisons use the same request triples and preserve their own target-Q definitions. The full-EM subset provides a within-library diagnostic under a larger physical budget; it is not an equally priced deployable baseline. A training-only nearest-neighbor retrieval baseline can provide an additional reference without treating lookup on a test library as independent prediction. These comparisons remain incomplete and their anticipated benefit is not reported as a result.

## Limitations and Conclusion

The present model study uses the frozen 6329-geometry development view, while the reported acquisition prefix uses the earlier 1804-row training baseline. These populations must not be pooled. Parent-family independence remains uncertified. Occupancy depends on grid resolution and does not prove joint attainability. Validation-selected comparisons are development results; the separately pre-frozen 1259 holdout evaluation does not justify further test-driven tuning or confer FINAL status. Shared-forward inverse dispersion omits uncertainty from retraining that surrogate, and bounded runs do not establish convergence. The historical64 pilot, development128 physical frame and 1259 SELF_PROXY holdout differ in model or target population and evaluation procedure, so their rates cannot establish a controlled improvement. The historical-reference pilot loses 35 extracted responses at the half-self-resonance gate; the development128 accounting separately retains 38 strict-invalid responses without assigning all failures an unverified common cause.

Even after the planned campaign, conclusions will remain specific to the recorded topology, process and simulation settings. The current work contains no measured-silicon, process-corner, mesh-convergence, broadband-synthesis or system-level matching validation. A 56-point sweep retained for validity checks does not convert a 15 GHz target task into a demonstrated broadband inverse-design method. Failed requests may arise from model, coverage, export, solver or specification limitations; the available evidence must determine which explanations are justified.

We have completed five-configuration, three-seed development training on 6329 geometries, the pre-frozen 1259 development holdout evaluation, the original 128-request physical frame for the frozen three-layer 256-unit seed-17 pair, a historical-reference 64-request physical pilot, an external 23-case forward diagnostic and a matched acquisition prefix. The development pair attains 40/128 original physical requests (31.25%), or 40/48 conditional on strict validity (83.33%), with zero pending; the historical inverse separately attains 17/64. The holdout inverse result 1253/1259 is SELF_PROXY only and is not pooled with either physical pilot. None of these observations establishes a final-model advantage, five complete forward-inverse physical rankings or completion of the 100000-qualified-geometry objective. Certified final partitions and the frozen 10000-request physical evaluation with its prechosen full-Q subset remain future work.
